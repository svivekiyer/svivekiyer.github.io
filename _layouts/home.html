Staff engineer · Storage platforms · London

I build the software that runs storage at petabyte scale.

Seventeen years in enterprise storage and data protection. The part that interests me is the half that is software — the platform an estate is actually operated through, the automation underneath it, and the distributed filesystems and hybrid-cloud namespaces it all runs on.

I write here about problems that keep recurring across vendors and estates, and publish runnable code for the ones that generalise.

What I work on

Four things, in roughly the order they take up my week.

Distributed filesystems and global namespaces

Parallel and scale-out filesystems at petabyte scale — cluster anatomy, quorum, declustered RAID, multi-tenant filesets — and global namespaces backed by object storage, where the real failure modes are cache coherency, cross-site asynchronous replication and a non-zero RPO. The appliance is the easy part. The distributed system underneath is not.

Storage platform engineering

Most storage teams operate their estate through whatever consoles the vendors shipped. I build the alternative: an authenticated platform in async Python over Elasticsearch, pulling a dozen independent systems — arrays, filers, backup, monitoring, ITSM, identity — behind one API, with per-source circuit breakers so an upstream outage surfaces as an error instead of as "nothing found".

Data protection and recovery

Backup and DR as a fleet platform rather than a nightly job — policy-driven protection across clusters and regions, immutability, restore validation, and failure triage that tells a missed cycle apart from a broken one. Plus the forensic side: reconstructing who deleted what, and when, from the audit record.

Data at scale

Over a hundred million filesystem objects indexed and billions of audit events made queryable, so capacity forecasting, ownership attribution and lifecycle decisions become measurements rather than opinions. Most storage estates cannot answer basic questions about their own contents. This is the work of making them answerable.
